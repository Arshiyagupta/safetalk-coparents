2.6 Resolution Maker Data (Paid)

Resolution Maker is a separate paid feature from the Message Coach described in §2.5. It works one arrangement at a time. You tell it what you and your co-parent are stuck on, it writes an arrangement, and you say yes or no. If you say no, you tell it what does not work, and the next arrangement is built from that. If you accept one and give us permission, we send that arrangement to your co-parent, who answers the same way.

If you use Resolution Maker, we collect and create:

  • What you are stuck on. Either a message you pointed at in your co-parent thread, or a sentence you typed to describe the disagreement.
  • Your reasons. Every reason you or your co-parent gives for turning an arrangement down, kept as it was typed.
  • Arrangements. Each arrangement the AI writes, and the one you both agree to if you get there.
  • Session state. Which arrangement is on the table, whose turn it is, how many arrangements have been written, and the AI's own working notes about each one.
  • Safety reads. Before each new arrangement, an automated check looks for indications of danger — a threat to hurt someone, a child being harmed, one parent controlling or frightening the other, someone talking about self-harm, or someone working around a protection ordered for a child. The check reads what you and your co-parent type inside Resolution Maker. It also reads recent messages between the two of you from your SafeTalk thread, which are included so the check can see the situation you are describing. When the check fires, the session ends, and we record what the check saw, in its own words, together with the exact words it was quoting. Those words may be yours or your co-parent's, and what the check writes down may describe either of you. This check is automated, and it is separate from the crisis detection described in §11.2. If you live in Washington State, §6.4 covers these records and what you can ask us to do about them.
  • Payment records. Identifiers that let us charge a card you saved — see §5.3.

What your co-parent sees, and what they do not. Your co-parent never sees the sentence you started with, the reasons you gave for turning an arrangement down, or the AI's working notes. The only thing that reaches them is the text of the arrangement itself, and only after you say yes to sending it. Their reasons are kept from you in the same way.

Where this lives. The session itself — your opening sentence, both of your reasons, the AI's working notes, and the safety reads — is stored on SafeTalk's servers in a place no app can read, yours or your co-parent's. What each of you sees on screen is written to a separate record that only that person can read.

Who at SafeTalk can see it. SafeTalk personnel can access Resolution Maker records for support, safety review, and engineering quality review, and to respond to legal process. As with everything else on SafeTalk, these records can be subpoenaed in a legal proceeding (§4.1, §5.2).

2.7 Session Replay and Product Analytics

To understand how the app is used and to find and fix problems, SafeTalk collects product-analytics data through two service providers, PostHog and Google Firebase Analytics. This includes which screens you open, which features you use, how long you spend, where you tap, device type, and app version. Through PostHog it also includes "session replay," a video-like playback of how the app's screens looked and how you moved through them. We use it to see what went wrong when something in the app breaks.

Session replay hides every piece of text and every picture on the screen before any replay data leaves your device. The words of your messages, your conversations with the coach, your children's names, and any photo or video shown in the app appear in a replay only as blank shapes. Documents you open from a message open in your phone's own viewer, outside SafeTalk, and are never part of a replay. SafeTalk does not run session replay or PostHog analytics on the screen that shows crisis resources.

PostHog stores this data in the United States. PostHog processes it only as SafeTalk's service provider, under a written agreement and on SafeTalk's instructions, and may not sell it, share it for advertising, or use it for its own purposes. SafeTalk does not sell this data or use it for advertising. Session replays are kept for 30 days and then deleted. Like other SafeTalk records, replay data could be requested in a legal proceeding while it exists.

Product analytics, including masked session replay, is a built-in part of how SafeTalk works and improves, and it is not optional. By creating an account and accepting the Terms of Service, you agree to it. If you do not want it, please do not use SafeTalk. Washington State residents and users in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland are treated differently, as described in Sections 6.4(h) and 6.5.

4.1 Permanent Record Retention

Unlike standard consumer applications, SafeTalk retains communication data indefinitely for legal and court-readiness purposes.

What We Retain Permanently:

  • ✓ All messages and conversations between co-parents
  • ✓ Mediation session history and AI-generated recommendations
  • ✓ Request records and response timestamps
  • ✓ Calendar events and modifications
  • ✓ Expense tracking records
  • ✓ File attachments and metadata
  • ✓ Complete audit trail with timestamps

  • ✓ AI-coached messages sent to your co-parent: These become part of the permanent co-parent communication record

Retention Period: Indefinite

Rationale:

  • Court-ready communications must be preserved for potential legal proceedings
  • Both co-parents have legitimate interest in maintaining complete records
  • Court orders may require indefinite record preservation
  • Legal statutes of limitations (often 7+ years) necessitate long-term retention
  • Records may be needed years after creation for custody, support, or other legal matters
  • Message Coach coaching conversations are retained because they form part of the communication record and may be discoverable in legal proceedings — including your messages to the coach, the coach's responses, and all draft iterations

Important: Your coaching conversations (your private messages to the AI coach) are retained alongside your co-parent communications. While coaching conversations are between you and the AI, they may be subject to subpoena or discovery in legal proceedings, including custody disputes. Different SafeTalk products have different deletion postures. Path to Resolution readings (§5.3) are deletable on request because they are personal analytical outputs, not shared communications. The indefinite-retention, deletion-resistant posture in this §4 applies to co-parent chat messages, mediation records, and other shared-communication artifacts; it does not extend to per-user analytical outputs.

4.3 Exceptions to Deletion Rights

Under GDPR Article 17 and CCPA, you generally have a right to request data deletion. However, these rights are LIMITED when:

  1. Court Order Exists: A court has ordered use of SafeTalk or preservation of records

  3. Legal Obligations: We must retain data to comply with legal or regulatory requirements
  4. Shared Data: Communications involve another user (your co-parent) who has equal rights to the data
  5. Legitimate Interests Override: The legitimate interest in preserving court-ready records outweighs your deletion request

Precedent: This approach is consistent with platforms like OurFamilyWizard and TalkingParents, which have established that court-focused communication platforms may refuse deletion requests due to the nature of the service.

Resolution Maker follows the indefinite-retention posture in this §4, not the Path to Resolution posture. A Resolution Maker session is not a personal analytical output. 11.2 Automated Crisis Detection; No Human Review; No Reporting Service

This section discloses how SafeTalk's automated crisis-detection feature handles your data.

SafeTalk uses automated detection to surface crisis resources when certain language patterns appear. No SafeTalk staff or contractor reviews these events. Crisis disclosures are not reported by SafeTalk to authorities — users are directed to contact 988, Childhelp, CPS, or 911 directly.

(a) What the system does. SafeTalk's automated pattern detection identifies language in coaching conversations that may indicate (i) imminent risk of self-harm or suicide, (ii) imminent risk of harm to a child, (iii) intimate-partner violence, or (iv) threats of violence against an identifiable person. When such patterns are detected, the system displays direct contact information for category-appropriate crisis resources, may pause or end the coaching thread, and logs the event as an event-only record (§2.5).

(b) What the system does not do. SafeTalk is not a crisis service, mental-health service, mandatory-reporting service, or emergency response service. No SafeTalk employee, contractor, or agent reviews crisis-detection events. SafeTalk does not contact emergency services, child protective services, law enforcement, or any third party on your behalf, and does not notify any other person that a crisis-detection event has occurred. Detection is automated; response is yours.

(c) Resources, not referrals. When a crisis-detection event fires, the system displays direct contact information for services equipped to help. Resource selection is category-specific, not collapsed into a single hotline:

  • For self-harm or suicide indicators: 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline);
  • For child-safety indicators: 1-800-4-A-CHILD (Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline) and a local Child Protective Services lookup specific to your state or country;
  • For intimate-partner violence indicators: 1-800-799-7233 (National Domestic Violence Hotline);
  • For imminent danger in any category: 911.

SafeTalk does not contact these services for you. If you or someone else is in danger, you must contact these services directly.
